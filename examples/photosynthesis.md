# Example: mechanism module (biology)

**Request:** `/explain why photosynthesis needs both light reactions and the
Calvin cycle`; target L2.

Begin with the problem: carbon fixation needs chemical energy and reducing
power, while light capture supplies them intermittently. Follow electron flow
to ATP/NADPH production, then show how the Calvin cycle consumes those carriers
to reduce carbon; explain why neither stage alone sustains sugar production.
Prerequisite bridge: ATP/NADPH are the cell's immediately usable energy and
reducing-power carriers; carbon fixation is the incorporation of CO2 into an
organic molecule.
Avoid saying only “plants convert light to food.” Give these checks before
revealing the key:

1. In bright light but with CO2 removed, predict ATP/NADPH production and the
   Calvin-cycle rate. Full credit requires “light reactions can initially make
   ATP/NADPH, but carbon fixation lacks substrate and slows; carriers may
   accumulate/recycle differently.”
2. If electron transport is blocked, predict which Calvin-cycle input is lost
   and why sugar production falls. Full credit requires naming ATP/NADPH and the
   causal dependency, not merely “the plant has no energy”.

Score each prediction for (1) naming the blocked carrier/step and (2) explaining
the causal consequence; map an error to electron-carrier production versus
carbon-fixation prerequisites. Progress record: concept/mechanism demonstrated
only after both predictions; otherwise schedule a new-condition retest.
