# Example: causal history module (history)

**Request:** `/chapter causes of the French Revolution`; learner knows dates
but not causal reasoning; target L2.

Do not present “economic, social, political” as independent buckets. Start
with the fiscal crisis and ask why a state unable to reform taxation faced a
legitimacy problem; connect institutional privileges, social conflict,
Enlightenment political language, and contingent harvest/war pressures as
interacting conditions. Distinguish long-run structure from short-run trigger
and flag interpretations as interpretations. Anchor the discussion in at least
one primary trace, such as *Déclaration des droits de l'homme et du citoyen*,
26 August 1789 (Articles 1 and 6), and a set of 1789 cahiers de doléances;
record the title/date and link when sources are available. Compare a
social-conflict interpretation with a cultural-political/revisionist reading,
state what each evidence base cannot establish, and label the causal story as
an interpretation of evidence rather than a directly observed fact. A check asks the learner to
compare two causal explanations, reconstruct what changes if the fiscal crisis
is removed, and identify what evidence would discriminate between them.
